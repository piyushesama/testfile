# Distinguishing planned chemotherapy omission from failure to receive intended treatment after hepatectomy

To the Editor:

Marino et al. distinguish early recurrence from loss of salvageability after resection of colorectal liver metastases.¹ They associate absence of adjuvant chemotherapy with futility and, citing Return to Intended Oncologic Treatment (RIOT),² suggest that perioperative care may be "futility-modifying". RIOT, however, depends on knowing what treatment was intended, which the report does not state.

In their cohort, 84.6% received chemotherapy before surgery and 15.9% after.¹ An untreated patient may have completed planned systemic therapy preoperatively, declined further treatment, developed complications that delayed it, or progressed or died before it could begin. These are different events with different remedies; only some reflect perioperative care.

We propose that hepatectomy series record the multidisciplinary treatment plan before surgery, update it after pathological review, and report whether intended treatment started, when, and, if not, why. Patients without planned postoperative chemotherapy should be reported separately from those unable to start it, and progression or death before treatment should be reported as such, not absorbed into one untreated group.

These categories would not establish benefit. Because futility is timed from surgery and chemotherapy starts later, receipt would still require landmark or time-dependent analysis. They would show how often and why intended treatment is not delivered. Until such data are reported, the association shows where to look, not that perioperative care modifies futility.
