On 2 November, v7 ("predictor or consequence?") went to Dr Genyk, Dr Moris and Amber Wilkes. Dr Genyk replied on 8 November:

v8 reframes the letter as a proposal: report planned omission of chemotherapy separately from failure to receive
intended treatment. This answers his question, and it is the right direction. As pasted, though, it had four weaknesses:

| Problem in v8 | Risk | Fix in v9 |
|---|---|---|
| It gives no fact from the paper; every point is hypothetical | Editors decline letters that do not "advance or clarify" or that are "mostly semantic" | Restores 84.6% preoperative against 15.9% postoperative chemotherapy, so "completed treatment before surgery" is grounded in their data |
| It misses that Marino invokes RIOT (Aloia 2014, their ref 25) and calls perioperative strategy "futility-modifying" | The strongest hook is lost | The letter now opens from their own framework: RIOT depends on knowing the intended treatment, and the paper does not report it. | "appropriate handling of treatment timing and confounding" is vague, and one sentence repeats paragraph 1 | Reads as filler | Names landmark or time-dependent analysis and states why (futility is timed from surgery, chemotherapy starts later); the repeated sentence is cut |
